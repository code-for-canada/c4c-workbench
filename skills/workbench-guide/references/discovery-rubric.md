# Discovery rubric

If you can, run the assessment in a separate agent that sees only the files, not the conversation. Read the discovery report and what it links to, then score each criterion: the lowest level the evidence supports, 1 if there's none, with a one-line note on the evidence. Write `discovery-assessment.html` with the scores and the single most useful next step, and link it from the report.

Discovery is ready for Alpha when every criterion is 3 or better, or the partner accepts a gap and it's recorded as a risk.

## Problem and outcome
- 1: The problem or intended outcome is unclear or framed as a predetermined solution.
- 2: The problem and outcome are named, but they remain broad or mostly assumed.
- 3: Evidence supports a specific user problem, intended outcome, and meaningful measures of success.
- 4: Evidence shows the problem's importance and scale. The intended outcome, baseline, and measures align with the organization's purpose.

## Users and wider journey
- 1: The users and their current experience are unclear.
- 2: Key users or journey steps are described, but the account relies mainly on assumptions or second-hand views.
- 3: Research explains key users' goals, behaviour, pain points, channels, and place in the wider journey.
- 4: Research includes relevant excluded groups and connected services. Findings materially changed the problem framing or scope.

## Constraints and viability
- 1: Material organizational, policy, technical, or operational constraints are missing.
- 2: Some constraints are listed, but their impact or evidence is unclear.
- 3: Evidence identifies the crucial constraints, capacity, costs, dependencies, and responsible decision makers.
- 4: Hard and changeable constraints are distinguished. Owners understand the changes, resources, and commitments required.

## Options considered
- 1: Discovery assumes one solution.
- 2: It considers a few similar options with limited evidence or comparison.
- 3: It compares genuinely different responses, including non-technology options, against user and organizational needs.
- 4: Credible evidence tests the main trade-offs. Feedback and research changed the shortlist or preferred direction.

## Direction and rationale
- 1: Discovery reaches no clear direction or reason to continue.
- 2: It names a preferred direction, but the rationale does not address evidence, alternatives, value, or cost.
- 3: The direction and scope follow from the evidence, expected value, constraints, and comparison of alternatives.
- 4: The rationale explains why to continue, change direction, or stop. It records evidence gaps and conditions that could change the choice.
