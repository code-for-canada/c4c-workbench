# Starting discovery

A good solution has to work on three fronts at once: **user desirability** (people actually want it), **technology feasibility** (you can realistically build it), and **organizational viability** (your organization can fund, run, and sustain it).

The trap is looking through only the lens you know best. Technical people can assume the tech is the whole game; policy people can fixate on organizational constraints. Either way, it's easy to miss the forest for the trees. Holding all three together is the goal of product management.

Most failed technology projects skipped this step: they designed a solution for how someone *imagined* the work happens, not how it actually happens. This activity builds the evidence base in three passes.

**End users** — the people the service exists for. What are they trying to get done, and where does today's process hurt? Just as important: *who gets excluded* by the current way of working.

**Other interest holders** — the staff who run the service, the people who can approve or kill a project, the technical folks, operations. A solution end users love can still die because a decision maker was never brought along or the operations team can't run it.

**Current technology** — the tools that hold the work today. Whatever gets built next has to live alongside (or replace) them, so we list them now, while it's cheap.

## Known vs. assumed

Throughout, we keep one honest distinction: what do we *know* (someone actually asked) versus what do we *assume*? Assumptions aren't failures — they become a research plan you can go run: who to talk to, what to ask, what answer would change our direction. Come back with what you heard and we'll fold it in.

## Further reading

- [**Understand users and their as-is process**](https://guides.18f.org/product/discover/users/) - Shows how to research, map, validate, and communicate the current user experience.
- [**Understand your key stakeholders**](https://guides.18f.org/product/discover/stakeholders/) - Identifies decision makers, affected groups, power dynamics, priorities, and engagement needs.
- [**Understand the technical landscape**](https://guides.18f.org/product/discover/technical/) - Inventories current systems, dependencies, risks, skills, contracts, costs, and maintenance capacity.
- [**Stakeholder and user interviews**](https://guides.18f.org/methods/discover/stakeholder-and-user-interviews/) - Gives a semi-structured interview method for understanding roles, needs, assumptions, and success measures.
- [**Contextual inquiry**](https://guides.18f.org/methods/discover/contextual-inquiry/) - Uses observation at work to uncover hidden needs and interactions between tools.
- [**System map**](https://guides.18f.org/methods/discover/system-map/) - Maps people, forces, relationships, evidence gaps, and opportunities within a complex system.
- [**Comparative analysis**](https://guides.18f.org/methods/decide/comparative-analysis/) - Structures an environmental scan of related agencies, services, patterns, gaps, and opportunities.
- [**User research in discovery**](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/user-research-in-discovery) - Covers likely users, current behaviours, pain points, service providers, evidence, and inclusive research.
- [**Map and understand a user's whole problem**](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/design/map-a-users-whole-problem) - Maps service landscapes and user experiences to expose gaps, duplication, dead ends, and ownership boundaries.
- [**Design with people and embed inclusion**](https://digital.gov.bc.ca/design/dcop/inclusion/) - Connects early research, journey maps, accessibility, inclusion, testing, and evidence-based decisions.
- [**OECD Good Practice Principles for Public Service Design and Delivery in the Digital Age**](https://engagement.oecd-opsi.org/engagement/processes/9/draft_versions/6) - Understanding user needs, building good services, sustaining technology teams.
- [**Microsoft Inclusive Design**](https://inclusive.microsoft.design/) - Identifies exclusion caused by mismatched experiences and uses human diversity to expose unmet needs.
- [**Digital Public Goods Standard**](https://www.digitalpublicgoods.net/standard) - Defines criteria for assessing open software, data, AI systems, and content during an environmental scan.
- [**Design hypothesis**](https://guides.18f.org/methods/decide/design-hypothesis/) - Links a proposed change to an expected outcome, measurable signal, experiment, and potential harm.
- [**Government of Canada Digital Standards**](https://www.canada.ca/en/government/system/digital-government/government-canada-digital-standards.html) - Frames success through user needs, iteration, accessibility, trust, stewardship, collaboration, and openness.
