# Jurisdictional Scan

Look outward at peer jurisdictions (or competitors) and inward at the partner's own existing tools.

## External: peer and best-in-class governments

For government partners, ask: what are other governments using to solve this problem?

- **Similar jurisdictions** — start with peers. A municipality should look at other municipalities of similar size and demographic profile; a federal department should look at federal counterparts in the Five Eyes / OECD; a province should look at other provinces and US states.
- **Best-in-class** — then look at jurisdictions known to be leading in digital government overall (e.g. UK, Estonia, Singapore, New Zealand, Canada Digital Service, US Digital Service / 18F, GOV.UK) or in this specific problem space.

## Internal scan

What solutions might be possible by using or integrating tools the partner already owns? Reason from anything known or inferable:

- Productivity suite already in use (Microsoft 365, Google Workspace).
- Existing line-of-business systems.
- Government-of-Canada shared services or whole-of-government tools (e.g. GC Notify, GC Forms, Canada.ca, Cloud Operations) for partners working within federal government (but not other orders of government).
- Province- or municipality-wide platforms.
- Recent vendor relationships or framework agreements that could be leveraged.

For each, capture:

> **[Tool / system](link if applicable)** — one short sentence on how it could be used and what the trade-offs are.
