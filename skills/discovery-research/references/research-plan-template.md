***Common activities:***

### **Get a Demo of the Existing Process**

In the spirit of show, don’t tell you can learn a lot very quickly about a service or a technology by seeing how the current product or process works. This can also help key interest holders become more familiar with the current experience. Ideally, you can record this for future reference.

**Who to involve:**

- The core project team
- Whoever is most familiar with the product or process
- The service’s decision-makers (or optionally, you can send them a recording)

### **Facilitate one-on-one discussions with key stakeholders**

Facilitate discussions with the service’s key decision-makers and internal stakeholders to gain an initial understanding about the service.

**Who to involve:**

- The project team
- The service’s decision-makers (e.g.: the business owner, product owner, etc.)
- The service’s key internal stakeholders (e.g.: the business lead/s, subject matter expert/s who manage the day-to-day operations of the service, the technical lead/s who support the service, etc.)

### **User Research**

Learning from users is a crucial way to gain a user-centric understanding of the service’s current state and future opportunities. Work with the team to connect directly with end users, or with external stakeholders who can act as a bridge between you and end users. Finally, reach out to end users to share an invitation to participate and an easy way for them to sign-up for your research activity.

**Who to involve:**

- The core project team
- (optional) Any members of the team who are interested in user research
- Member(s) of the team with connections to users and/or external stakeholders
- (optional) External stakeholders
