name: discovery-defining-success
description: Interview a user to elicit what a project's core vision of success looks like by defining a clear outcome you'd read in a future newspaper headline.
---

Surface the user's underlying motivation and their vision for what success looks like.

## What to ask
Ask questions as needed to figure out:

1. Tell us in your own words about your organization and what it does. Make sure you come away knowing where it sits — business, nonprofit, or government, which level and jurisdiction — since later requirements research depends on whose rules apply.
2. In their own words, what problem are they trying to solve? If the underlying *why* is superficial, unclear, or bakes in an assumed solution, that's ok, but ask thoughtful "why" questions 1–2 more times to get past the surface request.
3. What does success look like? *"If we pretend the project is wildly successful a year from now, what headline would be in the newspaper? Where would it be published?"*
4. Follow-up: *"What roadblocks would the article say you overcame?"*
5. What specific metrics and/or quotes would the reporter use as evidence of your success?

## Output draft
When you have a draft, write a simple but creative front-page newspaper-style `headline.html`. Include the intended outcome, evidence measures, and material roadblocks in the story. Don't quote the user verbatim — write a compelling but concise story with appropriate editorial construction. This is a newspaper, so it doesn't use the organization's branding.

## Discovery report
When you're done, update `discovery-report.html` as described in the `workbench-guide` skill.

## Teaching the method
If the user asks why this step matters, how it works, or says they're new to it, read `references/lesson.md` and explain it briefly in your own words, then return to the interview.
