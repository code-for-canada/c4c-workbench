# From what is to what could be

By now you should have a well-documented, clear understanding of the problem you're trying to solve, what your users need, and the constraints you need to follow.

Now the question is: what are all the possible solutions that could solve those problems?

The most obvious idea often preserves the current process or copies a familiar solution, but your desired outcome can be achieved in many different ways.

In this activity we do some broad brainstorming to explore the space of possible solutions before narrowing down to a few promising concepts worth putting in front of real people. The best part is, if we find that one concept doesn't work as expected, we can always come back and pick another approach!

## Further reading

- [**Define the future state**](https://guides.18f.org/product/define/) - Connects user and organizational needs to a shared vision, strategy, goals, and action plan.
- [**User scenarios**](https://guides.18f.org/methods/decide/user-scenarios/) - Turns user goals, motivations, context, and tasks into concrete stories for design decisions.
- [**Journey mapping**](https://guides.18f.org/methods/decide/journey-mapping/) - Visualizes interactions, decisions, emotions, harms, and opportunities across a service experience.
- [**Service blueprint**](https://guides.18f.org/methods/decide/service-blueprint/) - Connects user interactions with frontline actions, backstage work, systems, partners, and support processes.
- [**Design studio**](https://guides.18f.org/methods/discover/design-studio/) - Helps users, stakeholders, and team members generate, critique, combine, and prioritize different concepts.
- [**Design hypothesis**](https://guides.18f.org/methods/decide/design-hypothesis/) - Makes each concept's expected outcome, measurable signal, test, and potential harm explicit.
- [**Using scenario building to think through options for your service or policy**](https://designnotes.blog.gov.uk/2023/03/13/using-scenario-building-to-think-through-options-for-your-service-or-policy/) - Uses alternative future stories to expose trade-offs, consequences, assumptions, and design choices.
- [**Design with people and embed inclusion**](https://digital.gov.bc.ca/design/dcop/inclusion/) - Grounds requirements, roadmaps, journeys, and decisions in inclusive research and ongoing participation.
- [**Map and understand a user's whole problem**](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/design/map-a-users-whole-problem) - Designs improvement opportunities within the user's wider journey and the surrounding service landscape.
- [**Systemic Design Framework**](https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/resources/systemic-design-framework/) - Helps multidisciplinary teams explore, reframe, create, and catalyse change within complex connected systems.
- [**Create a Concept**](https://www.designkit.org/methods/create-a-concept.html) - Turns several promising ideas into concrete concepts that remain flexible enough for testing.
- [**Storyboard**](https://www.designkit.org/methods/storyboard.html) - Uses a low-resolution narrative to reveal how, where, and by whom a service concept gets used.
